## Known for

The canonical **macroeconomic** factor model. Tests whether pre-specified economic series
are priced risk factors, and identifies a standard set:

- industrial production growth (the real economy)
- unexpected inflation and changes in expected inflation
- the **term spread** (long minus short rates)
- the **default spread** (the risk premium)

Finds these are priced, while aggregate consumption and oil prices are not.

## Why it's here

⭐ **The pipes, named, in 1986.** If the article claims that being long a stock is partly a
position in rates, growth and inflation, this is the paper that made that claim testable and
identified which series matter.

Note the qualification carried by [[connor-1995]] and
[[merville-hayes-yelken-xu-2001]]: these forces are *real and identifiable* but explain
comparatively **little** return variance. The honest framing is that they are present and
small, not that they dominate.
