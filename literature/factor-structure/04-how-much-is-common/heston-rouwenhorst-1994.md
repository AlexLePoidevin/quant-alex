# Does Industrial Structure Explain the Benefits of International Diversification?

| | |
|---|---|
| **Authors** | Steven L. Heston; K. Geert Rouwenhorst |
| **Year** | 1994 |
| **Type** | Paper — *Journal of Financial Economics* 36(1), 3–27 |
| **BibTeX** | `heston1994does` |
| **Status** | To read |

## Known for

Decomposes international stock returns into **country** and **industry** components and
finds country effects dominated industry effects over their sample — cross-country
diversification was not simply a disguised bet on different industry mixes.

## Why it's here

Shows the factor taxonomy is not fixed by nature: whether "industry" or "geography" is the
organising force is an **empirical** question with a period-dependent answer (later work
finds the balance shifted toward industry). Supports treating the modes as a description of
a particular market at a particular time.
