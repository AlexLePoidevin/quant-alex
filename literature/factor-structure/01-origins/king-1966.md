## Known for

**The canonical market / industry / company decomposition.** Splits the variance of an
individual stock's return into a market component, an industry component, and a
company-specific residual, on US data from the 1920s–1950s. Widely cited for finding the
market component around half of a typical stock's variance and the industry component
roughly a tenth, with the remainder specific.

## Why it's here

⭐ **This is the paper for the layered picture of a single asset.** It is the earliest
rigorous statement that a stock is governed by *several kinds* of force at once — one
market-wide, one shared with its industry, one its own — and it puts approximate magnitudes
on each. Exactly the structure the article needs the reader to hold.

See [[campbell-lettau-malkiel-xu-2001]] for the modern version, which shows those shares
are **not stable over time**.
