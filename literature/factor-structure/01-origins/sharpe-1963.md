## Known for

**The single-index model** — the first statement that a security's return decomposes into a
common index plus a security-specific residual:

$$r_i = a_i + b_i I + \varepsilon_i$$

Motivated by computation, not economics: Markowitz's framework needs N(N−1)/2 pairwise
covariances, which was infeasible in 1963. The index model reduces that to N betas and N
residual variances.

## Why it's here

**Start the article here.** This is the origin of the entire idea, and the origin story is
instructive: factor structure entered finance as a *computational shortcut* and only later
acquired an economic interpretation. That ordering — approximation first, theory afterwards
— is worth telling.
