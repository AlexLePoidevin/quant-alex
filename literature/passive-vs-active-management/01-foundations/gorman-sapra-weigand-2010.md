## Key takeaway

For an active investor the relevant measure of risk is the **cross-sectional dispersion**
of returns, not time-series volatility. Dispersion raises total, systematic and
idiosyncratic risk — mostly idiosyncratic — so higher dispersion widens the opportunity to
earn active return. But the **information ratio is expected to be unchanged**: realized
tracking error scales proportionately with dispersion, cancelling the larger active return.

## Notes

- The IR result is the one to sit with: a high-dispersion regime is not automatically a
  better regime for a skilled manager, it is just a louder one. Pairs directly against
  IR = IC × √Breadth in Grinold & Kahn.
- The authors observe managers *reducing* active weights as dispersion rises.
