## Known for

Asks how much of an individual stock's movement is explained by systematic factors and
public news. The answer is **surprisingly little** — R² against market and industry factors
sits in the region of 0.2–0.35 for monthly individual stock returns, and excluding days with
identifiable news events barely improves it.

Roll's conclusion is that most individual stock movement is either private information or
"frenzy unrelated to concrete information."

## Why it's here

⭐ **The paper that keeps the article honest.** The natural opening claim — *you think you
bought Pepsi but you really bought the market* — is **wrong at the single-stock level**, and
this is the paper a reader would use to say so.

The correct argument is the inversion: each stock *is* mostly its own story, but the own-story
part is the part that **cancels** across holdings while the shared part does not. Roll
supplies the first half; [[campbell-lettau-malkiel-xu-2001]] supplies the second.
