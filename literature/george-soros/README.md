# George Soros

Primary-source material from Soros himself on **reflexivity** — the claim that participants'
imperfect views change the reality they are views of, so the causal arrow runs both ways
and the system has no belief-independent state to converge on.

Kept as a separate topic because these are *his own statements*, in his own framing, across
thirty years. The analytic apparatus lives elsewhere and is cross-referenced below.

## Cross-topic

- **[[soros-2013]]** — *Fallibility, Reflexivity and the Human Uncertainty Principle*,
  *Journal of Economic Methodology*. His one peer-reviewed statement of the argument, filed
  in `../market-structure/04-nonlinear-and-quantum/` because that is where it does work: it
  is the epistemological case for why a stationary factor covariance should be expected to
  fail. Left there deliberately rather than moved here.
- **`../growth-and-leverage/06-ergodicity/`** — the adjacent claim that the *time* average
  and the *ensemble* average differ. Distinct from reflexivity: ergodicity is about which
  average describes one participant, reflexivity about whether a stable distribution exists
  to average over at all. Both attack the same assumption from different sides.

## The standing problem with all of it

Reflexivity is stated, illustrated and asserted across every source here. It is nowhere
**operationalised**: there is no estimator, no test statistic, no falsifiable prediction.
That is the reason it has had far more influence on practitioners than on the literature,
and the reason a note in this folder can motivate a measurement but never substitute for
one.
