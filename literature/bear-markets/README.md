# Bear markets

Falling markets: how to define and date bull and bear markets, whether the regime can be called in time, the rallies and rebounds inside bear markets, how fear spreads across markets (risk-on/risk-off), and the history of bubbles, crashes and crises.

| `risk-on-risk-off` | How fear shocks (the VIX) move stocks, bonds and currencies together, correlation regimes, contagion across markets |

Many of the regime papers report backtests chosen in-sample; each note says how far the evidence goes. Volatility as a hedge is in `../tail-risk/` and `../volatility-and-derivatives/`, and trend following as crisis protection in `../tail-risk/defensive-strategies/` and `../momentum-and-trend-following/`.

## Bull and bear market regimes

- [[ang-timmermann-2012-regime-changes-and-financial-markets]]: Survey of Markov regime-switching models in finance: how a mixture of regimes produces fat tails, volatility clustering and changing correlations, what regimes do to equilibrium prices and asset allocation, and why estimated equity regimes separate calm from turbulent markets more than up from down.
- [[awoga-2018-momentum-market-regime-and-stocks-and-options-trading-strategies]]: A student capstone adding a hidden-Markov volatility-regime filter to moving-average and Black–Scholes-priced option strategies on the S&P 500; its headline returns come from full-sample regime fitting, synthetic option prices and no costs.
- [[bilokon-et-al-2021-market-regime-classification-with-signatures]]: Clusters sets of price paths by their path signatures and a kernel distance, letting the number of regimes emerge from eigenvalue gaps instead of being fixed in advance; demonstrated only on simulated Black–Scholes regimes.
- [[botte-bao-2021-a-machine-learning-approach-to-regime-modeling]]: Two Sigma fits a four-cluster Gaussian mixture to its factor returns and labels the clusters Crisis, Steady State, Inflation and Walking on Ice; a clear in-sample illustration, not a forecasting model.
- [[gonzalez-et-al-2006-defining-and-dating-bull-and-bear-markets]]: Bull and bear markets defined as persistent shifts in mean return and dated 1800–2001 with a Bry–Boschan rule and a count-cusum rule; bulls last about 21 months, bears 15–18, both longer than a random walk would give, and lagged signals cut risk in out-of-sample trading rules.
- [[haase-neuenkirch-2023-predictability-of-bull-and-bear-markets]]: Real-time forecasts of US bull and bear markets from 115 macro and financial series via factor-augmented Markov-switching models: 80%+ weekly accuracy and Sharpe 0.7–0.9 in a stocks/bonds switch (2004–2020), while return forecasts still fail to beat the historical mean.
- [[hoepfner-2018-retrospective-identification-of-bull-and-bear-markets]]: A one-parameter, after-the-fact dating rule for bull and bear markets (peaks and troughs within a centred window, window chosen for robustness), applied to six major indices 1992–2016; useful as an ex-post label, unusable in real time.
- [[li-zakamulin-2020-stock-volatility-predictability-in-bull-and-bear-markets]]: Volatility of 31 Dow stocks, 1998–2016, is forecastable much further ahead in bear markets (beyond 60 weeks) than in bull markets (about 12 weeks), with state-dependent bias; states are dated after the fact.
- [[maheu-mccurdy-2000-identifying-bull-and-bear-markets-in-stock-returns]]: A duration-dependent two-state Markov-switching model of monthly US stock returns, 1834–1995: a calm high-return bull state and a volatile low-return bear state, both becoming more persistent the longer they last, with the best bull-market gains in the first months.
- [[maheu-mccurdy-song-2021-bull-and-bear-markets-during-the-covid-19-pandemic]]: The four-state bull/bear Markov-switching model applied to 2020: bear from 26 February, bear rally from 22 April and still a bear rally in November because volatility stayed high; holding through forecast bear rallies and bull states beat buy-and-hold in 2020, the plain bull-regime rule did not.
- [[mcgee-2021-can-market-regimes-really-be-timed-with-historical-volatility]]: A replication of volatility-managed portfolios (Moreira and Muir) on the US market: the gain disappears without leverage, over most twenty-year windows, and when the monthly rebalance moves two weeks earlier; an out-of-sample volatility-regime switch does no better than buy-and-hold.
- [[srivastava-bhattacharyya-2018-dynamically-adaptive-systematic-trading-strategy]]: A student project splitting the S&P 500 (2000–2017) into four volatility-and-trend regimes and switching between technical rules for each; the 12.5% CAGR and 0.69 Sharpe come from full-sample regime labels and rules tuned on the same data.
- [[yang-wagner-2014-optimal-probabilistic-market-timing-using-bull-bear-cycle-statistics]]: A bull/bear switching rule from a practitioner firm: date cycles with gain, loss and duration thresholds, filter the probability of a bull regime, and trade on closed-form buy/sell thresholds; beats buy-and-hold mostly on drawdown, and the Golden Cross only narrowly out of sample.
- [[zegadlo-2022-identifying-bull-and-bear-market-regimes-rule-based]]: A one-parameter peak-and-trough filter for dating bull and bear markets in any asset class, compared on 12 stock, currency and commodity series with the Pagan–Sossounov and Lunde–Timmermann rules and a Markov-switching model; none of the rules reliably beats buy and hold.

## Bear market rallies and reversals

- [[boyarchenko-larsen-whelan-2023-the-overnight-drift]]: S&P 500 futures earn their largest hourly return at the European open (2–3 a.m. New York, about 3.6% a year), driven by overnight reversals of the previous day's closing order imbalance; reversals after selling are much larger than after buying, and larger when the VIX is high; not profitable after the bid-ask spread except as a conditional buy-the-dip.
- [[collin-dufresne-daniel-2015-liquidity-and-return-reversals]]: Daily reversal in the 100 largest US stocks, 1972–2014: about 10% of a non-news price shock is temporary and decays with a half-life of about 2.4 days, and once the strategy's own volatility is controlled for, the VIX does not predict reversal profits.
- [[goetzmann-kim-2018-negative-bubbles-what-happens-after-a-crash]]: 101 stock markets from 1692 to 2015: after a fall of 50% or more the next year is usually up and big rebounds are twice as likely as big further falls, while moderate declines tend to continue; not explained by macro conditions, crises, politics or survivorship.
- [[johannsen-2016-dead-cat-bounce]]: A prospect-theory model of the dead cat bounce: investors who sold at a loss after a bubble bursts all re-enter at about the same lower price to try to recover it, so demand jumps and prices rally temporarily; theory only.
- [[maheu-mccurdy-song-2012-components-of-bull-and-bear-markets]]: A four-state Markov-switching model of weekly S&P 500 returns, 1885–2010 (bear, bear rally, bull correction, bull): bear rallies recover about 7% of a 12% bear-state fall but with twice the volatility of a bull market, and most turning points pass through a rally or correction state.
- [[schultz-2002-bear-market-investing-strategies]]: A newsletter writer's Dow Theory guide to bear markets (2002): how bear markets differ from corrections, bear market rallies and legs, indicators, short selling and defensive tactics; rules of thumb rather than tested results.

## Risk-on, risk-off

- [[badshah-et-al-2018-asymmetric-linkages-fear-index-and-emerging-market-volatility]]: Quantile regressions and time-varying copulas, 2011–2016: VIX shocks pass to emerging-market, Chinese and Brazilian implied volatility mostly the same day, most strongly in the upper tail, and rises move them about twice as much as falls.
- [[barth-mitchell-sun-2020-runs-to-banks-cash-sweeps-during-market-downturns]]: When stocks fall, retail investors sell and brokerages sweep the cash into bank deposits: sweep deposit growth responds strongly to falling markets and little to rising ones (TD Ameritrade 2009–2020; +26.5% in March 2020), funding banks just as credit lines are drawn.
- [[caballero-kamber-2019-global-impact-of-risk-off-shocks-and-policy-puts]]: Weekly VARs on how a 15-point VIX shock moves stocks, yields and currencies worldwide, before and after the zero lower bound: unconventional policy still pulled long rates down and halved the shock's half-life, and emerging markets with strong fundamentals suffered least.
- [[dapena-serur-siri-2019-risk-on-risk-off-regime-switching-model]]: A hidden Markov model on the VIX-minus-realised-volatility spread switches between SPY and Treasuries (2004–2018, Sharpe 0.89 vs 0.46); no costs and a likely full-sample Viterbi look-ahead, so treat as an illustration.
- [[kinlaw-turkington-2014-correlation-surprise]]: Splits Kritzman–Li financial turbulence (a Mahalanobis distance) into magnitude surprise and correlation surprise, and shows that big moves with unusual co-movement are followed by higher volatility and, in US equities, lower returns.
- [[lopez-de-prado-2012-managing-risks-in-a-risk-on-risk-off-environment]]: Slides arguing that in a risk-on/risk-off market the danger is risk concentrated along one eigenvector of the covariance matrix, measured by portfolio entropy over eigenvectors, which correlations, regressions and risk parity can all miss.
- [[papenbrock-schwendner-2015-risk-on-risk-off-correlation-regimes-and-networks]]: Correlation regimes in 25 multi-asset futures, 1998–2013, found by clustering monthly correlation matrices: a post-2007 crisis regime collapses into bonds and yen against everything else, and a minimum spanning tree splits it into risk-on (centred on AUD) and risk-off (centred on the US 10-year note) branches.
- [[racicot-theoret-2018-multi-moment-risk-hedging-strategies-and-the-business-cycle]]: Hedge fund strategies' time-varying beta, co-skewness and co-kurtosis respond to VIX shocks and the business cycle: funds sell crash insurance in expansions, buy it in crises, and look more alike after volatility spikes.
- [[smales-2016-risk-on-risk-off-financial-market-response-to-investor-fear]]: How stocks, bond yields and currencies in the US, Australia and New Zealand move with the VIX, 2001–2015: a same-day flight to quality that strengthens in 2008–09, plus an untested suggestion to buy risk after fear spikes.
- [[smales-2022-spreading-the-fear-the-central-role-of-vix-in-global-uncertainty]]: Implied-volatility indices across the G7 and BRICS, 2001–2020: correlations rise in recessions and in COVID, and VIX changes lead the other indices one way (16–34% of their variance), partly a closing-time effect.

## Bubbles

- [[brown-dewey-2021-toil-and-trouble-dont-get-burned-shorting-bubbles]]: A practitioner look back at the 2005–08 short-subprime trade: the timing, carry, bailout, dealer-mark and counterparty risks that kept informed traders out, why it paid more than expected, and why buying after the crash was often the better trade (anecdotal, built from interviews).
- [[chiarella-he-zwinkels-2014-heterogeneous-expectations-in-asset-pricing]]: An estimated fundamentalist–chartist model of the S&P 500, 1970–2012: switching towards the more profitable rule improves the fit and produces long departures from fundamental value like the IT bubble.
- [[cividino-westphal-sornette-2023-multiasset-financial-bubbles-in-an-agent-based-model]]: An agent-based market with fundamentalists and herding momentum traders (an n-vector Ising model) in which bubbles appear when herding nears a critical level and, above it, cascade from one stock to the others because fundamentalists rebalance into them.
- [[fusari-jarrow-lamichhane-2025-testing-for-asset-price-bubbles-using-options-data]]: Detects bubbles from one day's option prices: fit a jump-diffusion model to puts, and a significant excess of market call prices over model values signals a bubble; rare in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 in 2014–18, more frequent in Amazon and Facebook, and present in the dot-com Nasdaq and GameStop.
- [[gurkaynak-2008-econometric-tests-of-asset-price-bubbles]]: A survey of econometric tests for rational bubbles (variance bounds, West, Diba–Grossman, collapsing and intrinsic bubbles): every test is a joint test with a model of fundamentals, and none can confirm a bubble.
- [[martin-ventura-2011-theoretical-notes-on-bubbles-and-the-current-crisis]]: A financial-accelerator model in which rational bubbles act as collateral that lets efficient firms borrow, so the 2008 crisis is read as a burst bubble that cut credit and output; government debt can replace the bubble only if it is credible.
- [[phillips-shi-2018-real-time-monitoring-of-asset-markets-bubbles-and-crises]]: A practical guide, with R code, to the Phillips–Shi–Yu real-time test for explosive bubbles and crashes, plus a bootstrap that controls false alarms; applied to the S&P 500 price–dividend ratio (1987, dot-com, 2008) and euro-area sovereign spreads.
- [[plantin-2023-asset-bubbles-and-inflation-as-competing-monetary-phenomena]]: A model in which a Taylor-rule central bank and sticky prices can produce 'monetary' bubbles that earn low returns, crowd out investment and burst when inflation returns; theory only.
- [[quinn-turner-2025-who-wins-and-loses-in-a-bubble-british-bicycle-mania]]: Named shareholder registers from the 1890s British bicycle mania: directors and industry insiders sold before the crash, and the investors left holding were wealthy gentlemen living near a stock exchange who traded heavily, not the naïve newcomers of bubble folklore.
- [[schatz-sornette-2020-inefficient-bubbles-and-efficient-drawdowns]]: A framework that defines a bubble as an explosive, inefficient run-up plus a crash at a random time (two types, depending on whether the full price is efficient), and argues rational-expectations bubbles cannot be tested.
- [[taleb-2021-bitcoin-currencies-and-fragility]]: Taleb's argument that a revenue-free asset with any risk of permanent abandonment is worth zero, and that bitcoin has failed as a currency, an inflation hedge and a safe haven; polemical, little data.
- [[tarlie-sakoulis-henriksson-2022-stock-market-bubbles-and-anti-bubbles]]: US valuation (log price over a CAPE-style fair value) modelled with a mean-reversion speed that can turn negative, defining bubbles and anti-bubbles as temporarily explosive valuation: five episodes in 1881–2017, triggered and ended by swings in expected profitability.
- [[west-1988-bubbles-fads-and-stock-price-volatility-tests]]: A 1988 survey of the excess-volatility tests: small samples do not explain the excess, rational bubbles fit poorly, and fads were plausible but untested.

## Crises and crashes

- [[bashchenko-marchal-2020-deep-learning-for-asset-bubbles-detection]]: Bubbles defined as strict local martingales and detected with a bidirectional LSTM trained on simulated prices (83.6% vs 49.7% accuracy against the rolling-window estimator); the Dow 2006–2008 long-short test is shown only as a chart and may use future data.
- [[blitz-2021-the-quant-crisis-of-2018-2020]]: Why equity factor strategies failed from June 2018 to August 2020: only the largest, most expensive growth stocks outperformed, profitability and momentum worked only as disguised growth exposure, and unlike earlier value drawdowns momentum did not offset the loss.
- [[blitz-2021-the-quant-cycle]]: A three-stage cycle for equity factors read off their own returns (normal, growth rally or value crash, reversal), which separates factor returns far better than recessions, inflation or ISM states, though it is dated by eye after the fact.
- [[brown-et-al-2015-model-risk-and-the-great-financial-crisis]]: A short history of model risk management in US banking, from LTCM's correlation assumptions and the Gaussian copula's failures in 2007–08 to the post-crisis explosion of stress-test models.
- [[bruner-carr-2007-the-panic-of-1907]]: A narrative history of the 1907 crash and banking panic, framed as a 'perfect storm' of seven converging forces, from system complexity and thin buffers to a real shock and failed collective action (local extract only).
- [[geithner-metrick-2018-ten-years-after-the-financial-crisis]]: Geithner's question-and-answer account of why the Fed saved Bear Stearns and AIG but not Lehman: lending against collateral could not stop a run on a nonbank, and only Congress could supply capital or guarantees.
- [[gormsen-koijen-2020-coronavirus-impact-on-stock-prices-and-growth-expectations]]: Dividend futures on the S&P 500, Euro Stoxx 50 and Nikkei 225 used to read the COVID-19 crash in real time: a model-free lower bound on growth revisions by horizon, point forecasts of dividend and GDP growth, and evidence that the crash and rebound were mostly about distant dividends and discount rates.
- [[jarrow-1992-market-manipulation-bubbles-corners-and-short-squeezes]]: Jarrow's model of when a trader big enough to move prices can profit risklessly with no information: corners and short squeezes always can, trend-and-sell strategies can if prices depend on the path of his holdings, and paper wealth can look like arbitrage when real wealth is not.
- [[kakushadze-2020-quant-bust-2020]]: Why dollar-neutral mean-reversion statistical arbitrage lost heavily in the COVID-19 selloff: long-only liquidations broke the industry correlations it bets on, making it effectively short deep out-of-the-money puts.
- [[khandani-lo-2011-what-happened-to-the-quants-in-august-2007]]: The August 2007 quant meltdown reconstructed from simulated factor portfolios and 5-minute data: a slow unwind of crowded value and momentum bets through July, two sharp unwinds on 1 and 6 August, market makers withdrawing from 8 August, and a near-full reversal on 10 August.
- [[lipton-lopez-de-prado-2020-three-quant-lessons-from-covid-19]]: A short essay from the COVID selloff: nowcast rather than forecast, build theories rather than backtest-mined rules, and replace all-weather strategies with regime-specific ones blended by nowcast regime probabilities.
- [[veraart-zhang-2021-a-macroprudential-view-on-portfolio-rebalancing-and-compression]]: Network theory of OTC-derivative post-trade risk reduction: dealer 'rebalancing' and compression cut variation-margin flows and procyclicality, but rebalancing can create new defaults because contagion depends on network shape, not gross notional.
