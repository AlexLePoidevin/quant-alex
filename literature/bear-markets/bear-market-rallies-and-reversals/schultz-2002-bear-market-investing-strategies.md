## What it is

A newsletter writer's practical guide to bear markets, written in 2001–02 after the dot-com
peak and the September 11 attacks. It works in the Dow Theory tradition (Hamilton and Rhea) and
covers:

- **Background:** what separates a bear market from a correction in a bull market, and a
  history of past bear markets in the Dow Jones Industrial Average.
- **Economic setting:** signs that a bull market is ending; foreign investment in a bear market.
- **Structure:** secondary reactions (bear market rallies) and bear market legs.
- **Tools:** technical indicators that work in both bull and bear markets, ones that change
  meaning in a bear market, cycles and chart reading.
- **Tactics:** preserving capital, short selling, hedging when you may be wrong about the
  direction, staying flexible, and defensive investments.
- **Psychology and outlook:** investor emotions, contrary opinion, and the author's forecast for
  the following years.

The chapters read closely here are the two on the shape of a bear market:

- **Secondary reactions (Ch. 6).** Every leg down in a bear market is interrupted by a rally
  against the trend, often in two or three phases. Rallies are faster and more violent than the
  primary moves; a three-week rally retracing 30–60% of a months-long decline is common. They
  start from oversold conditions and short covering, often without a visible base, and end as
  buyers near their cost price sell and shorts return. Low volume after a rally peak is a
  warning, and sentiment tends to be most bullish near the top of a bear rally. The common rule
  that rallies retrace one-third to two-thirds of the prior fall is quoted with Rhea's
  breakdown of how far secondaries went in his data.
- **Bear market legs (Ch. 7).** A bear market usually has three legs: euphoric expectations
  are abandoned, then earnings and the economy weaken, then people sell because they need the
  money. Each leg splits again into three. The author calls this the least exact part of the
  book.

## Why it's in the library

It is the practitioner vocabulary that the formal papers in this section test: **bear market
rallies as a normal part of every bear market**, distinct from the start of a new bull market,
and often mistaken for one. The Maheu, McCurdy and Song model turns the same idea into a
four-state probability model, and Johannsen offers a behavioural mechanism for it.

It is a book of rules of thumb, quotations and the author's experience, not tested results.
The retracement percentages and leg counts are offered without data or definitions that would
let you check them (Rhea's quoted shares of secondaries by size do not sum to 100%), and the
closing chapters are a forecast written for 2002.
