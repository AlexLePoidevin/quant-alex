## What it is

A narrative history of the 1907 US stock market crash and banking panic, written by a
business-school dean and a former journalist, organised around a general theory of why
crises happen.

- **The episode.** Of 13 US banking panics between 1814 and 1914, 1907 was among the
  worst: listed stocks fell 37% overall. The book traces it from the 1906 San Francisco
  earthquake (a real shock that drained gold and capital), through a failed corner in
  United Copper shares, runs on the Knickerbocker Trust and the Trust Company of America,
  a crisis on the stock exchange and in New York City's finances, to J. P. Morgan's
  private rescue, and on to the founding of the Federal Reserve. The chapter titles map
  this sequence.
- **The framework: a "perfect storm" of seven elements.** (1) a system-like architecture
  whose complexity hides what is happening and lets contagion spread; (2) buoyant growth
  that breeds mistakes; (3) thin safety buffers late in an expansion; (4) adverse
  leadership that raises uncertainty; (5) a real economic shock; (6) fear, greed and other
  behavioural swings that turn bad news into more bad news; (7) a failure of collective
  action, where the best-intended response is too small.
- **The stance.** Against both single-cause explanations (liquidity, greed, monetary
  policy) and lists of one-off local causes, the authors argue crises come from these
  forces converging, and that anticipating the convergence is the practical lesson.

## Why it's in the library

It is the historical case in the crises section for how a modest shock becomes a
system-wide panic without a lender of last resort, which is the mechanism later central
banks were built to stop. The seven-element framework is a useful checklist next to the
quantitative crisis and contagion papers here, although it is a narrative typology rather
than a tested model, and with seven loosely defined ingredients almost any crisis can be
made to fit it after the fact. Only the introduction is in the local file; anyone relying
on the specific lessons should read the book.
