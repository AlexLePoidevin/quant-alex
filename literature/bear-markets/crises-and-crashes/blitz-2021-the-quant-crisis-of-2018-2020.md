## What it is

A post-mortem of the multi-year drawdown in equity factor strategies from June 2018 to
August 2020, using Kenneth French's public factor and sorted-portfolio data (global
developed markets, US for the long history).

- **One way to win.** Of the 100 portfolios formed on size crossed with value, investment,
  profitability or momentum, 85 lagged the market over the 27 months, 70 of them by double
  digits. The winners were the largest and most expensive stocks: the mega-cap growth
  bucket beat the market by 53.8% (FAANG-type names and Tesla).
- **Profitability and momentum were growth in disguise.** Mega-cap top-profitability and
  top-momentum portfolios correlated 0.89 and 0.75 with mega-cap growth, and a constrained
  regression on the value-sorted mega-cap portfolios explains them fully. In a size × value
  × profitability sort, large growth stocks with the *weakest* profitability did best
  (+83.6%). Low-beta stocks beat high-beta ones within each size bucket, but
  only mid-beta mega-caps beat the market.
- **Why it was worse than earlier value drawdowns.** In the four big value drawdowns since
  1963 (1970–72, 1979–80, 1989–91, 1998–2000) momentum gains matched or exceeded value
  losses, and smaller stocks still offered places to outperform. 2018–2020 is the first in
  over half a century in which an equal mix of factors, or 50/50 value–momentum, failed to
  offset the value loss, and small caps underperformed across the board. The only other
  clear quant crisis in the sample is January–May 2009, a momentum crash that lasted under
  half a year.

## Why it's in the library

It is the clearest account of the 2018–2020 factor drawdown and of why diversifying across
factors did not help: the factors that "worked" were the same bet against value. It also
shows why long-only quant books, which structurally underweight mega-caps to get active
share, were hit hardest. It is descriptive and in-sample, the start and end dates are the
author's choice (he says results are robust to moving the end by a month or two), and it
was written by a quant manager in the middle of the drawdown, before value's 2021
rebound.
