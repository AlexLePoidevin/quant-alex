## What it is

A test of gold as a safe haven in 68 stock markets, with gold priced both in US dollars and
in each local currency. Monthly data, December 1999 to July 2021.

- **The identity.** Gold's local-currency return is roughly its dollar return plus the
  local currency's move against the dollar. When a currency falls in a crisis, gold priced
  in it rises by more than in dollars. When a currency rises, gold priced in it rises by
  less or even falls.
- **Averages.** Correlations between local stock markets and gold in local currency are
  negative in 54 of 68 markets (mean −0.06). Measured in US dollars for both, they are
  positive in 64 (mean +0.14).
- **In bad months.** Conditional on the worst 5% of stock months, local stocks and local
  gold are negatively correlated in 55 of 68 markets. At the worst 1% the split is 34–34
  and the mean is about zero.
- **The two crises.** Over four months of the 2008 crisis and of the COVID outbreak, gold
  rose in local currency for most countries: about +39% (2008) and +46% (COVID) in Brazil,
  +26% and +20% in Australia. It did not rise for investors in Japan, Switzerland and the
  US, or in currencies pegged to the dollar. Their currencies rose in the crisis themselves,
  and that outweighed gold's gain.
- **Bigger stock losses, bigger currency falls.** In extreme months, markets whose currency
  depreciated most also had the largest stock losses. The author reads this as capital
  flight, which also makes gold's protection in local currency outlast the roughly
  ten-day safe-haven effect seen in dollar prices.

## Why it's in the library

**Whether gold is a safe haven depends on the investor's currency.** For an investor whose
currency falls in a crisis (commodity, emerging-market and other risk-on currencies), gold
is a strong haven partly because it hedges the currency. For a dollar, yen or Swiss franc
investor the currency is already the haven, and gold adds little in the acute phase.

**It separates two properties that are usually blurred.** Gold as a currency hedge holds
on average. Gold as a safe haven only matters in the worst months. This paper shows the
first often produces the second for non-dollar investors.

**The evidence is weaker in the very worst months.** The conclusion that gold is a haven
"in more than 60 markets" rests on the average and 5% tail correlations. At the 1% tail the
evidence is evenly split, and monthly data blurs the short-lived dollar-price effect.
