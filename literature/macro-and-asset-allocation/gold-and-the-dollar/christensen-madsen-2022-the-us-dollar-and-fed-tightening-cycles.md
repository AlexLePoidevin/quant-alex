## What it is

A short bank note asking whether the dollar strengthens when the Fed raises rates. It
walks through the four hiking cycles from 1994 to 2018 using EUR/USD (DEM/USD for 1994).

- **1994–95 (+300bp).** A different FX regime, just after the 1993 European exchange-rate
  crisis. The D-mark outperformed the dollar.
- **1999–2000 (+175bp, the shortest and smallest cycle).** EUR/USD fell from 1.20 to below
  0.90. The authors attribute this to doubts about the new euro, not to the Fed; concerted
  intervention stopped it in late 2000.
- **2004–06 (+425bp, the largest).** The dollar weakened throughout. The dominant driver
  was concern about the US trade deficit, which took EUR/USD from below 0.90 in 2002 to
  above 1.50 by 2008.
- **2015–18 (+300bp, the longest).** EUR/USD fell before the first hike and then rose from
  below 1.05 to 1.25 after an optimistic Draghi speech in 2017, despite five Fed hikes.
- **Conclusion.** No cycle shows a reliable dollar rally from Fed hikes. The authors say
  "every cycle is different" and the dollar follows whatever the dominant theme is. They
  nonetheless forecast dollar strength for 2022 (EUR/USD to about 1.08), on the view that
  the US–euro rate gap would be the driver this time. (EUR/USD did fall much further than
  that in 2022, to below parity, so the call was right in direction.)

The second half of the note is a weekly preview: the FOMC meeting, PMIs, US consumer
confidence and PCE inflation, and Swedish data.

## Why it's in the library

It is a quick, sourced counterexample to "Fed hikes mean a strong dollar". Over four
cycles the dollar's direction was set by other themes: the euro's launch, the US trade
deficit, ECB policy. Four cycles is not a sample, though: the note is a narrative with
charts, not a test, and it comes from a bank's sales desk.
