## Time-series momentum and trend

- [[babu-et-al-2020-trends-everywhere]]: Out-of-sample time-series momentum on 82 assets never used in the original studies (EM equity, swaps, EM currencies, exotic commodities, CDS indices, volatility futures) and 16 equity factors, 1985–2017: gross Sharpe 1.17 traditional, 1.34 alternative, 0.95 factors, 1.60 combined, with alpha over matched passive volatility-scaled portfolios.
- [[babu-et-al-2020-you-cant-always-trend-when-you-want]]: AQR splits a trend strategy's Sharpe ratio into the size of market moves, trend efficacy and diversification, and finds the weak 2010–2018 decade is explained by small market moves rather than a broken strategy.
- [[baltas-kosowski-2017-demystifying-time-series-momentum-strategies]]: What the volatility estimator, the trend rule and cross-asset correlation each do to a futures trend strategy, 1974–2013: a significant-trend rule cuts turnover by two thirds at the same Sharpe ratio, and cutting leverage when correlations are high doubles the weak 2009–13 Sharpe ratio (0.14 to 0.29).
- [[benhamou-2016-trend-without-hiccups-a-kalman-filter-approach]]: Four Kalman-filter state-space models used as a low-lag trend signal on E-mini S&P futures; the one-year backtest is fitted in-sample with up to 15 parameters, so the headline profits are illustrative only.
- [[bruder-et-al-2011-trend-filtering-methods-for-momentum-strategies]]: Lyxor's survey of trend estimators behind momentum strategies (moving averages and crossovers, Hodrick–Prescott, Kalman as an exponential moving average, L1 filtering, wavelets, multivariate trends), with calibration methods and the warning that a filtered trend is a noisy estimate whose predictive value must be tested.
- [[cfm-2018-the-convexity-of-trend-following]]: CFM's derivation that a moving-average trend rule is mechanically convex in the asset it trades, and how diversification, position caps and signal smoothing trade that convexity for Sharpe ratio (the SG CTA index fits a parabola in the S&P 500 with R² of only 0.18); a manager's note.
- [[dai-et-al-2016-optimal-trend-following-trading-rules]]: Proof that in a market switching unobservably between bull and bear, the optimal long/flat strategy buys when the filtered bull probability crosses an upper threshold and sells below a lower one, with a simulation and an S&P 500 test over 1972–2011.
- [[fung-hsieh-2001-the-risk-in-hedge-fund-strategies-trend-followers]]: Trend-following funds modelled as owners of lookback straddles: straddle portfolios on bonds, currencies and commodities explain about half the variance of CTA returns (adjusted R² 47.9% against about 1% for asset-class indices) and their positive payoffs in equity crises.
- [[hoffstein-sibears-2018-diversifying-the-what-how-and-when-of-trend-following]]: A practitioner case that a simple US equity trend rule hides three uncompensated risks — which asset, which signal and lookback, and which rebalance week — with rebalance week alone moving $1 to anywhere from $4,139 to $6,797 since 1927; the fix is to diversify across each.
- [[jusselin-et-al-2017-understanding-the-momentum-risk-premium]]: Amundi's continuous-time model of trend following: the P&L splits into a convex option profile and a trading impact, giving positive skew, a hit ratio under 50%, negative vega, opposite diversification preferences for time-series and cross-sectional momentum, and protection against gradual declines but not jumps.
- [[moskowitz-ooi-pedersen-2012-time-series-momentum]]: The paper that named time-series momentum: each of 58 futures markets' own past 12-month return predicts its next month, a diversified volatility-scaled portfolio has a Sharpe ratio above 1 over 1985–2009 with a straddle-like smile against the market, and speculators profit from it at hedgers' expense.
- [[rahelan-2019-taking-the-stag-out-of-stagflation]]: Trend-following signals applied to equity factors in 23 countries (1999–2019) add nothing after costs (Sharpe 0.45, information ratio about zero), and a stagflation-conditioned version claims Sharpe 1.03, but its predictor was selected in the same sample from ex post recession and inflation dates.
- [[roncalli-2017-keep-up-the-momentum]]: Roncalli's plain-language account of trend following as a convex, long-straddle payoff: driven by the size of Sharpe ratios, correlations and moving-average length, best held as a bad-times diversifier, with time-series momentum wanting uncorrelated assets and cross-sectional momentum correlated ones.

## Cross-sectional and asset-allocation momentum

- [[asness-et-al-2014-fact-fiction-and-momentum-investing]]: AQR and Moskowitz answer ten common objections to stock momentum with Kenneth French's data: 8.3% a year gross over 1927–2013, split evenly between long and short sides, present in large caps, and best held alongside value (60/40 mix Sharpe 0.80, worst drawdown −30% against −77% alone).
- [[blitz-huij-martens-2011-residual-momentum]]: Momentum built on stock-specific (Fama–French residual) returns instead of total returns: similar profit at about half the volatility, so roughly double the Sharpe ratio, and far smaller losses in 1932, 2000–2009 and recessions.
- [[geczy-samonov-2017-two-centuries-of-multi-asset-momentum]]: Cross-sectional momentum from 1800 to 2014 in country equities, currencies, bonds, sectors, US stocks and across asset classes (combined 0.45% a month, t = 15.4), with commodity spot prices mean-reverting instead, decade-long losing stretches, market-state-dependent betas and record-high correlations between momentum strategies by 2014.
- [[medhat-schmeling-2022-short-term-momentum]]: Last month's stock return reverses among thinly traded stocks (−16.9% a year for winners minus losers) but continues among heavily traded ones (+16.4%), a short-term momentum that persists for a year, sits in large liquid stocks, survives estimated costs (1.00% a month net) and holds in 22 other developed markets.
- [[nadler-schmidt-2019-momentum-strategies-for-etf-based-portfolios]]: Winner and loser deciles across ETF universes and sector-ETF holdings, 2007–2017: one-month reversal and one-year continuation hold mainly in a multi-asset ETF set, with significance overstated by overlapping daily-entry returns.
- [[resolve-2019-global-equity-momentum-a-craftsmans-perspective]]: ReSolve's robustness test of dual-momentum Global Equity Momentum across 1,225 lookback and signal variants: no variant beats the others, the original 12-month rule fell back to the median out of sample, and averaging all variants gives the same return with shallower drawdowns.

## Momentum crashes and risk

- [[butt-virk-2020-momentum-crashes-and-variations-to-market-liquidity]]: Momentum crashes after down markets coincide with months when market liquidity improves and illiquid past losers rally; the same-month liquidity change subsumes much of the market-rebound and variance explanations, but explains crashes after the fact rather than predicting them.
- [[fan-et-al-2022-momentum-and-the-cross-section-of-stock-volatility]]: Momentum crashes start at selection: picking winners and losers by raw return loads both legs with volatile assets that have no momentum, and dividing past return by volatility to a power (about 2–3) raises Sharpe ratios and cuts crash losses in UK stocks, commodities and equity indices.
- [[garg-et-al-2021-momentum-turning-points]]: Why time-series momentum on the stock market places bad bets around turning points: blending 1- and 12-month signals into four observable phases (bull, correction, bear, rebound), volatility timing as part of the alpha, and a dynamic rule that slows down after corrections and speeds up after rebounds.
- [[hutchinson-obrien-2014-is-this-time-different-trend-following-and-financial-crises]]: Trend following over 1925–2013 earns 4% a year in the two years after a global financial crisis begins against 13.6% otherwise, because futures returns stop autocorrelating in crises; the weakness lasts about four years and shows up in regional crises too.
- [[kessler-scherer-2013-momentum-and-macroeconomic-state-variables]]: Cross-asset momentum over 1984–2010 (Sharpe ratio about 1 at 1% volatility) earns most in recessions by going long bonds and short equities, and its alpha disappears once factor exposures are allowed to vary with macro state variables.
- [[swedroe-2020-left-tail-risk-and-left-tail-momentum]]: A blog summary of the left-tail momentum anomaly: US stocks with high recent value-at-risk underperform (−0.94% a month, 1962–2014) because investors underreact to persistent large losses.

## Machine learning for trend

- [[beaudan-he-2019-logistic-regression-for-momentum-based-trading-strategies]]: A daily S&P 500 in-or-out rule from a regularised logistic regression on polynomial combinations of momentum and drawdown: Sharpe 0.54 against 0.35 for the price index over 1964–2018, but before costs, on the price index only, and with degree and horizon chosen on the reported period.
- [[chang-chang-chou-2022-the-trend-is-your-friend-ensemble-learning]]: A stacked ensemble (SVM, MLP, AdaBoost, XGBoost under a decision tree) predicting uptrend/downtrend days for Taiwan's top-50 ETF: the most accurate single models lose about 30%, the stack returns 19% against 11% for buy-and-hold, on one 540-day test period with 31 trades.
- [[huddleston-liu-stentoft-2021-intraday-market-predictability-a-machine-learning-approach]]: Machine-learning forecasts of five-minute S&P 500 returns from the lagged returns of its 500 constituents, 1993–2016: real but mostly pre-decimalisation predictability, with a thin edge that survives the quoted spread in some models after 2005.
- [[kim-kim-2021-deep-asset-allocation-for-trend-following-investing]]: Long-only stock trend portfolios in the S&P 500, FTSE 100 and KOSPI 200 weighted by past-year return beat equal weighting, and autoencoder-based return forecasts add a little more; the training design as written may include each forecast's own target.
- [[li-deng-2007-machine-learning-approach-to-predict-turning-points]]: Turning-point prediction as classification: a chaos-motivated embedding of past prices, a radial basis function network trained on a forward 'turning indicator', and a validation-chosen threshold, shown on TESCO and the Dow with figures only and no benchmark.
- [[sethi-2016-applied-machine-learning-for-systematic-equities-trading]]: A UCL PhD building a short-horizon US equity strategy: momentum-or-reversal signals from Kaufman's efficiency ratio fed to an RBF network with a no-trade class, PageRank-style Markov-chain weights for the stocks that signal, and online learning for intraday order timing; the backtests carry survivorship and per-stock annualisation problems.
