## What it is

A short note on how far the best long-term stocks fell along the way.

- **Concentration.** US stocks added over $47 trillion of wealth over Treasury bills from
  1926 to 2019, but 83 firms (0.32% of the total) account for half of it and five firms for
  11.9%.
- **Method.** For each of the seven decades since 1950, the note takes the 100 firm-decades
  that created the most shareholder wealth and measures the largest peak-to-trough fall in
  total return between month-ends within the decade, and within the decade before.
- **Drawdowns in the good decade.** Even in their best decade these stocks fell 32.5% on
  average, over drawdowns lasting about 10 months. Apple created $1.47 trillion in
  2010–2019 and still fell 39.5% from September 2012 to June 2013.
- **Drawdowns in the decade before.** In the preceding decade the same stocks fell 51.6% on
  average, over about 22 months. Microsoft fell 63.3% in 2000–2009 before its $1.08 trillion
  decade; Citigroup fell 97.0% before its 2010s recovery.
- **Examples.** Apple had three drawdowns over 70% between 1983 and 2003, including 75.6%
  in four months of 2000. Amazon fell 91.3% from February 2000 to September 2001 (93.1% from
  March 1999).

## Why it's in the library

- **Drawdowns are normal even for the winners.** A 30–50% fall says little about whether a
  holding will turn out well. Any rule that cuts positions after large losses will also cut
  many of the eventual winners.
- **Concentration makes this matter.** If most wealth comes from a few stocks, missing them
  through a stop-loss or a drawdown limit is costly.
- **What it does not show.** The sample is chosen with hindsight: these are the stocks that
  turned out best, so the note says nothing about how often a 50% drawdown is followed by a
  recovery in general. It is descriptive, with no test, and the funder is a long-horizon
  growth investor whose approach the message suits.
