# Tail risk

Crashes: what protecting a portfolio against them costs and pays, defensive strategies that hold up over long bad periods, why markets can be calm for years and then fall faster than the calm-period data suggest, and what actually happens inside a crash.

## Tail hedging

- [[ambrus-capital-2025-marketing-vs-math-solutions-based-vs-alpha-focused-tail-risk-hedging]]: A volatility manager's case that calendar-rolled tail hedges bleed (equity and volatility risk premia, compounding of the bleed, paying wide spreads, fitting exits to a handful of crashes) and that hedges should be traded on price instead; the diagnosis is checkable, the cure is asserted without a track record.
- [[bhansali-2018-right-tail-hedging-managing-risk-when-markets-melt-up]]: A tail-hedging manager's case for buying calls against a melt-up, with a jump-diffusion example of why one possible up-jump makes calls dearer than Black–Scholes and can push implied volatility up as the market rises; one hand-picked trade, no backtest.
- [[chang-holdom-bhansali-2021-tail-risk-hedging-performance-measuring-what-counts]]: Why compounded NAV returns misstate an option hedge whose payoffs are withdrawn when they happen: a put programme showing −99.99% NAV return lost 49% in dollars, and as an S&P 500 overlay cut drawdown from −57% to −45% for 0.6 points of return; the manager's own backtest.
- [[doran-2020-volatility-as-an-asset-class-holding-vix-in-a-portfolio]]: Why cash VIX would be an excellent equity hedge but VIX futures, calls and VXX are not, which SPX options actually drive VIX (in-the-money puts), and a cheap synthetic VIX hedge from a put spread and a short call spread, switched off when VIX is high.
- [[luo-dash-2011-vix-futures-and-the-hedging-of-bond-portfolios]]: S&P on its own VIX futures index as a hedge for bond portfolios, 2005–2011: useless for Treasuries, weak for investment grade, strong for emerging-market debt; a VIX-above-25 switching rule improves returns and drawdowns, in a short back-filled sample.
- [[szado-2009-vix-futures-and-options-portfolio-diversification-during-the-2008-crisis]]: Adds long VIX futures and calls to equity, 60/40 and institutional portfolios through the 2008 crash: 10% in VIX futures cut an August–December loss from 20% to 4%, and VIX calls beat cost-matched SPX puts; a case study whose window ends at the bottom.
- [[szado-2020-portfolio-diversification-potential-of-long-vix-futures-and-options]]: Small long-VIX futures and call positions added to a 60/40 and an endowment portfolio, 2006–2017: big gains in 2008, a steady drag from contango otherwise, VIX calls compared with S&P 500 puts, and the VXTH dynamic tail-hedge index.

## Defensive strategies

- [[barro-canestrelli-lanza-2014-volatility-vs-downside-risk]]: A multi-period optimisation that layers volatility control and tail-loss control on an index-tracking portfolio, compared with a rule-based volatility target in three 50-week windows (2005–2010); small-sample and cost-free.
- [[bessembinder-2020-extreme-stock-market-performers-expect-some-drawdowns]]: The 100 firm-decades that created the most US shareholder wealth since 1950 still fell 32.5% on average within their best decade and 51.6% in the decade before; even the biggest long-run winners have deep drawdowns along the way.
- [[bhansali-holdom-2021-diversifying-diversification]]: A manager's test of six crash hedges (franc, yen, gold, bond futures, trend, a put ladder) over 1971–2020 drawdowns: an equal-risk basket of hedges was as good as an optimised one and more stable, though spot currencies, no costs and modelled option prices flatter it.
- [[clare-et-al-2017-reducing-sequence-risk-using-trend-following-and-the-cape-ratio]]: Perfect withdrawal rates for 20-year retirements on US equities since 1872: a 10-month moving-average filter halves drawdowns and lifts sustainable withdrawals most in bad cases (10th percentile 7.2% vs 5.0%), and CAPE helps set withdrawals in some periods.
- [[dugan-greyserman-2019-the-impact-of-skew-on-performance-and-bias]]: A simulation study showing that negatively skewed return streams look better over short windows but suffer roughly twice the long-run maximum drawdown of positively skewed ones at the same Sharpe ratio, with negative skew amplifying the drawdown cost of volatility.
- [[granger-et-al-2014-rebalancing-risk]]: The original argument that rebalancing to fixed weights equals buy-and-hold plus a short straddle on relative performance, which deepened 60/40 drawdowns in 2001–02 and 2008–09, and that a trend overlay offsets it.
- [[hamill-et-al-2016-trend-following-equity-and-bond-crisis-alpha]]: Futures trend following 1960–2015, including the bond bear market before 1985: consistent returns, positive skew like a long straddle, and best performance in the worst equity and bond markets, mostly from short lookbacks.
- [[harvey-et-al-2018-the-impact-of-volatility-targeting]]: Volatility targeting across 60+ assets since 1926: Sharpe ratios improve only for equities and credit (through the leverage effect), but left tails shrink everywhere, and balanced and risk-parity portfolios improve from 0.80 to 0.91.
- [[harvey-et-al-2019-the-best-of-strategies-for-the-worst-of-times]]: Man Group's scorecard of crisis hedges over the eight worst S&P 500 drawdowns and three recessions, 1985–2018: puts reliable but cost 7.4% a year, bonds depend on a historically unusual negative stock–bond correlation, gold and credit protection in between, and trend plus beta-neutral quality as cheaper dynamic hedges.
- [[harvey-et-al-2020-strategic-risk-management-out-of-sample-evidence-from-covid-19]]: Man Group's out-of-sample test of its crisis-protection rules in the February–March 2020 selloff: fast trend and profitability long-short held up, volatility targeting roughly halved the equity drawdown, strategic rebalancing helped a little, and moving-average crossovers lagged simple momentum in a fast crash.
- [[hoffstein-faber-braun-2020-rebalance-timing-luck-of-smart-beta]]: How much a factor portfolio's result depends only on which months it rebalances: 1–4% a year of 'timing luck' for 100-stock semi-annual portfolios, a 41.7-point one-year gap in an S&P Enhanced Value replication, and staggered tranches as the fix.
- [[hong-meyer-brauns-2021-portfolio-rebalancing-tradeoffs-and-decisions]]: Dimensional's comparison of calendar, tolerance-band and tiered rebalancing for 60/40 portfolios: returns barely differ, so choose on turnover against tracking error, where bands (especially rebalancing only to the band edge) beat the calendar.
- [[ilmanen-et-al-2021-tail-risk-hedging-contrasting-put-and-trend-strategies]]: Index puts versus multi-asset trend following as crash hedges, 1985–2020: puts lose money in every decade but pay in every bad month; trend earns a positive return and was up in nine of the ten worst S&P 500 months, because the worst month is rarely the first of its drawdown.
- [[mcquinn-thapar-villalon-2021-portfolio-protection-its-a-long-term-story]]: AQR's case that long bad periods hurt wealth more than sharp crashes, that puts protect only against the latter, and that defensive equities, risk parity, styles and trend do better over long bad outcomes for 60/40; partly by construction, as the alternatives are scaled to fixed Sharpe ratios.
- [[molyboga-swedroe-qian-2020-short-term-trend-a-jewel-hidden-in-daily-returns]]: Time-series momentum on 78 futures from daily data, plus a weekly-rebalanced 21-day trend sleeve that diversifies slow trend in its worst periods; the gain survives only at optimistic trading costs.
- [[neville-et-al-2021-the-best-strategies-for-inflationary-times]]: Which assets and strategies held their value in 34 inflation surges across the US, UK and Japan since 1926: equities and bonds lost in real terms together, commodities and trend following were the reliable protection, and TIPS were robust but expensive.
- [[pedersen-2021-does-volatility-harvesting-really-work]]: Thousands of random US stock portfolios, 2007–2021: equal-weight rebalancing barely beats buy-and-hold outside the 2008–09 crash and recovery, losing a little in declines and gaining in rebounds, before costs.
- [[rattray-et-al-2020-strategic-rebalancing]]: Why monthly rebalancing deepens 60/40 drawdowns in trending sell-offs, and two fixes tested over 1960–2017: a 10% trend allocation (about 5 points off the average worst drawdown) or delaying rebalancing while the trend is negative (2–3 points).
- [[sepp-2018-trend-following-strategies-for-tail-risk-hedging-and-alpha-generation]]: When trend following is convex to the S&P 500: only when returns are measured over longer than the signal's half-life, so trend hedges slow bear markets but not fast crashes; plus the 2011–18 slump as negative autocorrelation and a 50/50 risk-parity/trend mix that halved drawdown in 2003–18.
- [[van-hemert-et-al-2020-drawdowns]]: How likely a given maximum drawdown is (43% chance of −20% over 10 years at 10% volatility and Sharpe 0.5), what drives it, and when drawdown-based firing or de-risking rules beat total-return rules.

## Volatility and market structure

- [[ambrus-capital-2022-is-the-vix-becoming-increasingly-leptokurtic]]: What the VIX actually measures, why it stayed subdued during 2022's orderly 20% decline, and Ambrus's untested argument that short-dated options and dealer concentration are fattening its tails.
- [[ambrus-capital-2022-volatility-and-the-changing-market-structure]]: Ambrus's list of structural reasons US equities have become calm-then-crash fragile (dealer gamma hedging, few option market makers, Dodd–Frank risk limits, structured products, passive and leveraged ETFs, common VaR triggers), and its case for a small managed volatility allocation.
- [[ambrus-capital-2023-dispelling-false-narratives-about-0dte-options]]: Who trades zero-day SPX options and in which direction: mostly institutions selling volatility at the open, which damps markets day to day, with the risk that one-sided put buying amplifies a shock (classification method proprietary).
- [[barbon-buraschi-2021-gamma-fragility]]: Barbon and Buraschi measure option dealers' gamma imbalance stock by stock (2010–2020): when dealers are short gamma, volatility and intraday momentum rise, more so in illiquid stocks, and flash crashes are somewhat more likely and markedly deeper.
- [[cole-2017-volatility-and-the-alchemy-of-risk]]: Christopher Cole's essay arguing that roughly $1.5tn of strategies that size risk by volatility (risk parity, volatility control, risk premia, short-VIX products, and buybacks) form one giant short-volatility trade that can turn a shock into a crash, with Black Monday 1987 as the template.
- [[cole-2018-what-is-water-in-markets]]: Cole's follow-up to the 2017 essay: February 2018 as a liquidity crisis rather than a volatility event (E-mini depth down 95%, tail puts driving the VIX), a toy simulation of passive investing crowding out active price discovery, and a macro forecast that half came true.
- [[gayda-et-al-2022-option-liquidity-and-gamma-imbalances]]: SPX option market makers' aggregate gamma inventory built from Cboe open-close data, 2004–2020: spreads widen when dealers are short gamma, and dealers flatten their gamma when markets are illiquid, volatile and intermediaries are constrained.
- [[hood-et-al-2019-risk-parity-is-not-short-volatility]]: AQR's simulation test of the claim that risk parity is a hidden short-volatility trade: their daily trades are uncorrelated at every horizon, and after removing market exposure short volatility explains under 1% of risk parity's variance.
- [[squeezemetrics-2020-the-implied-order-book]]: SqueezeMetrics on dealer gamma (GEX) and vanna (VEX) exposure as a hidden S&P 500 order book, and why customer-sold puts that go in the money can turn dealer hedging into a crash feedback loop; vendor argument from scatterplots.

## Market microstructure and flash crashes

- [[baldacci-et-al-2020-options-market-making-in-high-dimension]]: A stochastic-control method for a dealer quoting many options on many underlyings: a value function quadratic in inventories cuts the problem to a few PDEs, with greeks updated continuously; tested only in simulation.
- [[bouchaud-kockelkoren-potters-2006-random-walks-liquidity-molasses-and-critical-response]]: Why prices stay a random walk despite long-memory order flow: each trade's impact decays as a power law tuned to a critical value, because liquidity providers lean against the flow (a 'liquidity molasses'); when that liquidity steps back, crashes follow. Paris Bourse trade data, 2002.
- [[demos-et-al-2015-some-statistical-properties-of-the-mini-flash-crashes]]: Return statistics around 14 single-stock mini flash crashes (2011–2012) and the 2010 Dow: non-normal, power-law tails, chaos and log-periodic fits tried and then doubted, threshold GARCH fits but misses the tails.
- [[easley-et-al-2011-microstructure-of-the-flash-crash]]: Easley, López de Prado and O'Hara on 6 May 2010 as a liquidity crash: order-flow toxicity (VPIN) in E-mini futures rose for hours before the fall as high-frequency market makers withdrew; the early-warning claim is the inventors' own and has been disputed.
- [[filippou-et-al-2022-no-max-pain-no-max-gain]]: Tests the options-folklore 'max pain' effect on US stocks, 1996–2021: in the week before expiry, prices drift towards the strike where listed options pay out least (about 0.4% a week on a decile spread), with no effect in matched stocks that have no options.
- [[flora-reno-2020-v-shapes]]: A test (the V-statistic) for prices that overshoot and reverse, separating market failure from fast but efficient repricing; applied to the 2010 flash crash, SPY through Covid, and a €434m loss on Italian bond auctions in May 2018.
- [[kim-et-al-2021-investigation-of-flash-crash-via-topological-data-analysis]]: Persistence landscapes of one-minute joint returns on S&P, Dow and Nasdaq futures, turned into an anomaly score that spikes on 6 May 2010; one event, no false-alarm rate.
- [[kirilenko-et-al-2018-automation-intermediation-and-the-flash-crash]]: CFTC account-level E-mini data on 6 May 2010: high-frequency traders and market makers never held more than about 6,000 contracts net against a 75,000-contract sell program, and HFTs traded like quote snipers rather than market makers.
- [[poitras-2012-what-happened-may-6-2010-anatomy-of-the-flash-crash]]: A market historian's reading of the SEC–CFTC reports on the 6 May 2010 flash crash next to the 1987 crash, arguing that futures selling passed to stocks by index arbitrage, outside the collateral limits on short selling, drove both.
- [[romero-2016-microstructure-noise-two-scales-realized-volatility]]: A thesis walk-through of the two-scales realized volatility estimator (Zhang, Mykland and Aït-Sahalia): why summing squared tick returns measures bid-ask noise rather than volatility, how TSRV removes it, and a small inconclusive test of whether wider-spread Dow stocks carry more noise.
- [[sarkissian-2019-statistical-mechanics-of-price-stabilization-and-destabilization]]: A Landau-style free-energy model of order imbalance in which small repricings are absorbed as a random walk but large ones trigger a phase transition (correlation and volatility spike, order flow goes one-sided), with a tanh price-impact formula; a phenomenological sketch rather than a tested model.
- [[shi-et-al-2020-modeling-flash-crash-behavior-with-multivariate-hawkes-processes]]: A multivariate Hawkes model of the 30 Dow stocks through the 6 May 2010 Flash Crash: fewer but much stronger cross-stock links, cross-excitation catching up with self-excitation, and clusters that cut across sectors.
- [[tee-ting-2019-cross-section-of-mini-flash-crashes]]: A Kalman-filter detector for mini flash crashes in S&P 500 trade data, 2010–2013: 7,492 crashes at z = 6, clustered on news and event days, with smaller, more volatile, thinner and lower-priced stocks moving furthest.
- [[wikipedia-2013-vpin-flow-toxicity]]: A Wikipedia overview of the probability-of-informed-trading models, from the 1996 PIN model to volume-clock VPIN with bulk classification, and the uses claimed for it; good for definitions, one-sided on evidence.
- [[zakamulin-giner-2022-optimal-trend-following-in-a-markov-switching-model]]: Proves that in a two-state bull/bear Markov switching model the exponential moving average is the optimal trend-following rule, gives its window in closed form, and estimates a 9-month EMA for the S&P 500 from 1926–2020 data.
