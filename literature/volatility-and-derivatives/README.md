# Volatility and derivatives

Volatility as something you can trade, and the options models used to price it: the VIX and
its futures, variance and volatility swaps, the variance risk premium, option pricing models
beyond Black–Scholes, the FX smile, and hedging exotic options with vanillas. Many of these
are Peter Carr's papers, with co-authors.

| Folder | What's in it |
|---|---|
| `vix-and-volatility-indices` | How the VIX is built and where it is biased, VIX futures and VXX, the futures term structure, and joint VIX–SPX models |
| `variance-and-volatility-swaps` | Replicating variance with an option strip, volatility and corridor swaps, options on realised variance, dispersion and correlation trading |
| `volatility-risk-premia-and-forecasting` | The variance risk premium, the leverage effect and volatility feedback, forecasting realised variance, and volatility regimes |
| `option-pricing-models` | Lévy and variance gamma models, stochastic volatility by time change, local Lévy, implied-volatility surfaces and PDEs, option P&L attribution |

| `exotic-options-and-static-hedging` | Barriers and digitals, static and robust replication with vanillas, super-replication bounds, and bond convexity |

Several sources here are bank research or practitioner slides, and some are working-paper
versions of journal articles; each note says which. How volatility instruments behave in a
crash, and dealer gamma as an amplifier, are in `../tail-risk/`. Stochastic calculus
background is in `../mathematical-foundations/`.

## VIX and volatility indices

- [[avellaneda-et-al-2021-trading-signals-in-vix-futures]]: A VAR model of the VIX futures curve (constant-maturity futures and roll yields) and a neural network that picks one-month/five-month calendar positions by expected utility; strong cost-free cross-validation results that largely disappear with realistic costs.
- [[avellaneda-papanicolaou-2018-statistics-of-vix-futures-and-applications-to-trading]]: A stationary two-factor model of the VIX futures curve that explains why ETNs rolling long VIX futures (VXX) bleed and shows that the short side earns only a modest, highly variable Sharpe ratio.
- [[brenner-et-al-2007-the-market-for-volatility-trading-vix-futures]]: The first years of VIX futures (2004–2006): upward-sloping futures curve, futures volatility falling with maturity, and a mean-reverting variance model that prices the futures within about 2%.
- [[carr-madan-2014-joint-modeling-of-vix-and-spx-options]]: One closed-form model (double-gamma VIX², variance-gamma index) fitted jointly to SPX and VIX options at a single maturity, used to hedge a call on realized volatility with listed SPX and VIX options.
- [[carr-wu-2006-a-tale-of-two-indices]]: What the old (VXO) and new VIX measure: VIX² is a 30-day variance-swap rate built from an option strip; plus 1990–2005 evidence on the variance risk premium (short variance swaps averaged +40% a month with heavy negative skew), FOMC effects and bounds on VIX futures.
- [[farooqui-2020-a-note-on-trading-the-term-structure-of-vix-futures]]: A short note linking the VIX futures curve to dealer risk appetite and claiming a term-spread switching strategy with twice the benchmarks' Sharpe ratio, built on an undisclosed signal and so impossible to verify.
- [[gehricke-zhang-2018-modeling-vxx]]: A Heston-based model of the VXX note showing that its near-total decay is the roll yield of VIX futures, driven by the negative market price of variance risk, which can be backed out from VXX's return.
- [[kucharczyk-et-al-2018-neural-networks-and-arbitrage-in-the-vix]]: How the VIX is computed and settled from hundreds of SPX options, and an LSTM that tracks minute-by-minute VIX moves from just ten of them (61% directional accuracy over two months, barely ahead of a random forest).
- [[lin-lin-2009-hedging-effectiveness-of-vix-futures]]: Delta-vega hedges of a short S&P 500 futures call: VIX futures beat a forward-start strangle at hedging forward volatility (2004–05), and Greeks from a stochastic-volatility-with-jumps model hedge best.
- [[osterrieder-et-al-2019-the-vix-volatility-index-a-very-thorough-look]]: A replication of the VIX from raw CBOE quotes: what drives the number, how CBOE's approximations bias it low (up to 7.5%), which levers could move it, and why the settlement auction is a different calculation from the index.

## Variance and volatility swaps

- [[avellaneda-2009-dispersion-trading]]: Avellaneda's lecture on trading index options against component options: the implied-correlation relation, intrinsic-value hedges, the split of gamma P&L into idiosyncratic and dispersion terms, and a weighted Monte Carlo signal model with early-2000s backtests.
- [[bloomberg-2018-variance-swaps]]: Bloomberg's documentation of how its OVML pricer values (mainly FX) variance swaps: 1/K² replication with a flat-volatility control variate, Greeks after fixing starts, and the effect of the notional currency.
- [[bossu-carr-papanicolaou-2022-static-replication-of-standard-dispersion-options]]: Any smooth payoff on the Euclidean dispersion of several assets can be replicated by a continuum of vanilla basket calls, so dispersion options have a model-free price given basket option prices.
- [[bossu-strasser-guichard-2005-just-what-you-need-to-know-about-variance-swaps]]: The standard practitioner introduction to variance swaps: the contract, the convexity premium in the strike, forward-volatility and dispersion uses, why a delta-hedged option is a gamma-weighted variance swap, and the 1/K² option-strip replication with its dividend and jump corrections.
- [[carr-et-al-2005-pricing-options-on-realized-variance]]: Options on realized variance and volatility priced in pure-jump (CGMY) models calibrated to the smile, showing that the Brownian clock is a poor proxy for quadratic variation once returns jump.
- [[carr-et-al-2011-options-on-realized-variance-and-convex-orders]]: Whether options on realized variance, scaled to unit mean, get more valuable with maturity as stock options must: Lévy models say no (averaging narrows the distribution), a few 2009 market quotes hint yes, and a Lévy-exponential variance model guarantees it.
- [[carr-et-al-2017-pricing-variance-swaps-on-time-changed-markov-processes]]: When jumps break the 'variance swap = two log contracts' rule: the swap is priced by a European payoff G that depends only on the Markov dynamics, never on the stochastic clock carrying the volatility.
- [[carr-laurence-2011-multi-asset-stochastic-local-variance-contracts]]: Breeden–Litzenberger and Dupire extended to baskets: basket-call prices give the joint risk-neutral density (via the Radon transform), and variance of a portfolio paid only while prices sit in a chosen region can be priced and hedged without specifying volatility.
- [[carr-lee-2003-trading-autocorrelation]]: Carr and Lee's model-free contracts on whether markets trend or revert: covariance swaps (fair rate zero), hyper options (worth the same as Europeans), up/down-crossers, and why option values need not rise with variance.
- [[carr-lee-2007-realized-volatility-and-variance-options-via-swaps]]: Pricing and hedging options on realised variance, realised volatility and the VIX from variance- and volatility-swap prices (or synthetic swaps built from vanillas); the gap between the two swap rates gives the volatility of volatility.
- [[demeterfi-et-al-1999-more-than-you-ever-wanted-to-know-about-volatility-swaps]]: Goldman Sachs's derivation of variance-swap replication with a 1/K²-weighted option strip (the basis of the VIX formula), with closed forms for the effect of skew, the failures from a limited strike range and from jumps, and why volatility swaps need a model for volatility of volatility.
- [[dupire-2006-model-free-results-on-volatility-derivatives]]: Dupire's slides on what can be said without a volatility model: a model-free upper bound on VIX futures (the convexity gap to forward variance), skew as evidence of volatility of volatility, business-time delta hedging, and a lower bound for calls on realized variance.
- [[huang-torne-2021-sticky-varswaps]]: Extends Bergomi's skew-stickiness ratio from at-the-money volatility to the variance-swap strike, with local-vol and Heston approximations, showing the wings of the smile can move with spot differently from the at-the-money point.
- [[itkin-carr-2010-pricing-swaps-and-options-on-quadratic-variation]]: Variance and volatility swaps on discretely sampled returns under time-changed Lévy models: closed-form strikes, a correction to the log-contract price when sampling is sparse, and a finding that smile-calibrated models still miss the variance-swap term structure.
- [[jacquier-slaoui-2010-variance-dispersion-and-correlation-swaps]]: Why a dispersion trade's implied correlation differs from the correlation-swap strike: the dispersion P&L is a correlation spread plus a volga (vol-of-vol) term that accounts for the gap.
- [[lee-2008-corridor-variance-swap]]: Roger Lee's three-page entry on corridor variance swaps (up- and down-variance): definition, model-free replication with options inside the corridor, Fourier pricing, and why a variance swap replicated with a limited strike range is really a corridor swap.
- [[rolloos-2018-model-free-pricing-and-hedging-of-forward-starting-volatility-swaps]]: The zero-vanna rule extended to forward-starting volatility swaps, with an approximate model-free hedge in zero-vanna forward-starting straddles scaled by a skew adjustment.
- [[rolloos-arslan-2015-taylor-made-volatility-swaps]]: A volatility swap's fair strike is approximately the implied volatility at the zero-vanna (d₂ = 0) strike, immune to spot–vol correlation to first order and close to exact Heston prices.
- [[roring-2017-volatility-and-variance-swaps]]: A master's thesis that walks through variance-swap replication and the volatility-strike convexity gap, then compares implied volatility, a vanna-vomma estimator, EWMA and GARCH as forecasts of S&P 500 realised volatility (GARCH best; implied volatility sits above realised).

## Volatility risk premia and forecasting

- [[beber-et-al-2013-switching-risk-off-fx-correlations-and-risk-premia]]: Risk-off episodes dated from switches in G10 FX correlation regimes: carry loses sharply when correlations jump to the high regime, and whether volatility hurts carry depends on which way the regime is switching.
- [[carr-wu-2009-variance-risk-premia]]: Carr and Wu measure the variance risk premium as realized variance minus a variance swap rate synthesised from an options strip, and find it strongly negative for US indexes (a long 30-day S&P 500 variance swap lost about two-thirds of its cost on average, 1996–2003) and largely unexplained by CAPM or Fama–French factors.
- [[carr-wu-2016-analyzing-volatility-risk-and-risk-premium-in-option-contracts]]: Models the implied-volatility surface's near-term moves directly (the vega-gamma-vanna-volga model), reduces the surface to a quadratic in a few states, defines per-option realized and expected volatility, and extracts a risk premium that predicts S&P 500 returns out of sample.
- [[carr-wu-2017-leverage-effect-volatility-feedback-and-self-exciting-market-disruptions]]: Splits the negative return–volatility link into financial leverage, volatility feedback and self-exciting crashes, estimated on 1997–2014 SPX options from one month to five years; the negative variance risk premium comes from the diffusion and crash channels.
- [[carr-wu-zhang-2019-using-machine-learning-to-predict-realized-variance]]: Ridge and ReLU-network regressions on SPX option prices to forecast realized variance; predicting the gap to a VIX-style benchmark works best, and few near-the-money options nearly match VIX's full strip.
- [[doman-doman-2020-daily-fx-volatility-and-the-time-of-day]]: GARCH estimates of daily volatility for four USD pairs, bid and ask, computed from each of the 24 hourly closes (2003–2019): the estimate depends on the hour, most around market opens and closes, enough to move VaR.
- [[marabel-2011-volatility-regimes-for-the-vix-index]]: A two-regime Markov-switching model for the monthly level of the VIX with Student-t errors and ARCH/GARCH variance, identifying multi-year low- and high-volatility regimes from 1990 to 2010.

## Option pricing models

- [[carr-2003-option-pricing-using-integral-transforms]]: Slides surveying Fourier pricing: the Carr–Madan FFT formula, Lévy processes specified by their characteristic exponents, and stochastic volatility by time change with bond-pricing Laplace transforms.
- [[carr-et-al-2003-stochastic-volatility-for-levy-processes]]: NIG, variance gamma and CGMY run on a CIR clock to add stochastic volatility, fitted to a year of S&P 500 option surfaces (CGMYSA fits best).
- [[carr-et-al-2004-from-local-volatility-to-local-levy-models]]: Dupire's local volatility generalised to a Lévy process run at a price- and time-dependent speed, with formulas for recovering that speed from option prices.
- [[carr-et-al-2007-time-for-a-change-variance-gamma-model]]: A plain-language derivation of the variance gamma model from random news arrivals, and how it fits the S&P 500 smile in Bloomberg's SKEW function.
- [[carr-et-al-2019-model-free-pdes-for-implied-volatility]]: Quasilinear parabolic (and first-order hyperbolic) PDEs that Black–Scholes implied volatility itself satisfies, solved by finite differences to get a whole grid of implied vols at once; about 40× faster than a point-by-point Matlab root-finder, with caveats.
- [[carr-faguet-1996-valuing-finite-lived-options-as-perpetual]]: American options approximated in closed form by treating time decay as Poisson credit downgrades of a perpetual option (the 'randomisation' or Canadization trick), with Richardson extrapolation giving penny accuracy in three steps.
- [[carr-wu-2010-stock-options-and-credit-default-swaps]]: One affine model for a firm's stock options and CDS: default intensity loads on stock variance plus an independent credit factor, which explains part of the single-stock put skew as priced default risk and identifies recovery rates.
- [[carr-wu-2011-a-new-simple-approach-for-constructing-implied-volatility-surfaces]]: Slides: model each option's implied volatility directly and the whole surface becomes the root of a quadratic; fits SPX and FX surfaces better than Heston and calibrates about 100 times faster.
- [[carr-wu-2020-option-profit-and-loss-attribution-and-pricing]]: Prices an option from its daily Greek P&L: the fair implied volatility balances theta against gamma, vega, vanna and volga, so skew and curvature become forecasts of spot–vol covariance and vol of vol.
- [[monciaud-reghai-2021-singular-exotic-perturbation]]: A fast formula for how much local-stochastic volatility changes an exotic's price relative to local volatility, written in terms of exotic vanna and volga (the risk left after vega-hedging with vanillas) and tested on autocalls.

## FX options and the smile

- [[austing-2011-repricing-the-cross-smile]]: An analytic joint density for two FX pairs that reprices the smiles of both pairs and of their cross, built by plugging smile volatilities into the Black–Scholes best-of formula, with no-arbitrage conditions on a triangle of smiles.
- [[bakshi-et-al-2008-stochastic-risk-premiums-and-skewness-in-currency-options]]: Global and country-specific risk premiums estimated from options on the dollar–yen–pound triangle: frequent jumps, only downside jumps priced, and stochastic skew in the FX smile.
- [[bossens-et-al-2010-vanna-volga-methods-applied-to-fx-derivatives]]: FX smile quote conventions and the Vanna-Volga pricing rule for barriers and touches, with survival-probability and first-exit-time damping, tested against dealer quotes alongside local and stochastic volatility.
- [[carr-fisher-ruf-2014-on-the-hedging-of-options-on-exploding-exchange-rates]]: When an FX model makes the exchange rate a strict local martingale, pricing by replication under both currencies' measures restores put-call parity and prices the chance that one currency collapses.
- [[carr-kakushadze-2017-fx-options-in-target-zone]]: Pricing FX options when a central bank holds the rate in a band: no-arbitrage forces reflecting boundaries and an interest-rate differential that depends on where the rate sits in the band, and narrow bands allow prices as simple trigonometric series.
- [[carr-wu-2007-stochastic-skew-in-currency-options]]: Eight years of OTC USD/JPY and USD/GBP smiles: stable curvature but risk reversals that swing and change sign, captured by two time-changed Lévy components with separate clocks.
- [[castagna-mercurio-2007-the-vanna-volga-method-for-implied-volatilities]]: Castagna and Mercurio's standard account of vanna-volga: how FX desks build a whole smile from three quotes by pricing the cost of hedging vega, vanna and volga with the 25-delta put, ATM and 25-delta call, with closed-form weights, consistency results and implied-volatility approximations.
- [[credit-suisse-2019-target-redemption-forward]]: Credit Suisse's two-page client sheet on the target redemption forward: a zero-premium strip of FX forwards at an enhanced strike, cut short once the client's gains hit a target, with leveraged notional on the losing fixings.
- [[fuligni-2020-fx-options-for-the-practitioner]]: A Credit Suisse practitioner's deck that walks through FX options from vanillas to correlation products: greeks including vanna and volga, barrier rules of thumb and replications, why FX skew exists, volatility and variance swaps, and correlation swaps, dual digitals and worst-ofs.
- [[garrivier-2020-fx-vanilla-pricing-and-smile-calibration]]: A working paper on FX vanilla conventions (spot, forward and premium-adjusted deltas, market versus smile strangles) and two ways to calibrate one smile slice from the five market quotes, delta interpolation and SVI, compared on six-month USD/JPY.
- [[guyon-2013-a-new-class-of-local-correlation-models]]: Local (state-dependent) correlation models that exactly fit a basket or FX cross-rate smile, built with the particle method; on the EUR/USD–GBP/USD–EUR/GBP triangle, admissible correlations still leave a min-of-calls price anywhere from 2.37 to 2.91.
- [[janek-2011-the-vanna-volga-method-for-derivatives-pricing]]: A thesis deriving the exact and Wystup's simplified vanna-volga methods, extending them to barrier options with survival-probability weights, and testing them against Heston and market quotes on EUR/USD and EUR/PLN.
- [[reiswich-wystup-2010-a-guide-to-fx-options-quoting-conventions]]: How FX options are actually quoted: four premium conventions, spot/forward and premium-adjusted deltas, the several at-the-money definitions, and how to turn delta-volatility quotes into strikes.
- [[reiswich-wystup-2012-fx-volatility-smile-construction]]: How to build an FX implied-volatility smile from the quoted ATM, risk-reversal and strangle volatilities: the delta and ATM conventions, why the quoted strangle is a price condition, and a parabola-in-delta smile that calibrated in all but 6 of 30,000+ daily fits.
- [[shevchenko-2006-implied-correlation-for-pricing-multi-fx-options]]: Implied correlation between two exchange rates from FX option volatilities: the currency-triangle formula, and its extension to rates with different quote currencies for multi-currency barrier options.
- [[wystup-2008-vanna-volga-pricing-pros-and-cons]]: The vanna-volga rule for pricing FX barriers and touches from the risk-reversal and butterfly overhedge, with reverse-barrier hedging effects, FX implied correlation from the currency triangle, and basket and structuring exercises.
- [[wystup-2017-fx-options-and-structured-products]]: Wystup's practitioner book on currency options, covering FX quotation and delta conventions, vanilla combinations, first- and second-generation exotics, structured products such as TARFs and PRDCs (with case studies of trades that went wrong in 2008), hedge accounting and vanna-volga pricing.
- [[wystup-et-al-2020-fx-derivatives-stochastic-local-volatility-slides]]: Why FX exotic pricing moved from vanna-volga to stochastic-local volatility: vanna-volga's inconsistencies, SLV calibration with a leverage function and mixing factor, and mixture local volatility as the faster market-standard compromise.

## Exotic options and static hedging

- [[allen-2001-managing-options-risk-for-exotic-options]]: Course notes on marking and controlling exotics by the cost of a hedge in liquid instruments: replicating European payoffs with vanillas, pin risk and reserves for binaries, and the Derman–Ergener–Kani, Carr and optimisation static hedges for barriers.
- [[carr-chou-1997-breaking-barriers]]: Carr and Chou's static hedge for barrier options: any payoff as bonds, a forward and a strip of vanilla options weighted by its second derivative, and a reflected 'adjusted payout' that replicates a barrier claim with European options in Black-Scholes.
- [[carr-ellis-gupta-1998-static-hedging-of-exotic-options]]: Carr, Ellis and Gupta's put-call symmetry and the static hedges it gives for barrier, double-barrier, roll-down, ratchet and lookback options using only vanilla puts and calls, with bounds when carry is non-zero.
- [[carr-itkin-muravey-2022-barrier-options-in-the-time-dependent-heston-model]]: A generalized-integral-transform method for barrier options in a Heston model with time-varying parameters and barrier, reducing the problem to a 2D integral equation; accuracy against finite differences ranges from a few percent to 40%.
- [[carr-lorig-2015-robust-replication-of-barrier-style-claims]]: Pricing and hedging claims on price and realised variance, including single and double barriers, with only the underlying and same-expiry vanillas, assuming volatility independent of returns; examples include realised-Sharpe options and barrier claims on leveraged ETFs.
- [[carr-wu-2014-static-hedging-of-standard-options]]: A long-dated option spanned by a fixed portfolio of shorter-dated options weighted by its future gamma; with jumps, three to five options beat daily delta hedging in simulation and on 1996–2002 SPX options.
- [[chen-et-al-2008-static-super-replicating-strategies-for-exotic-options]]: The model-free upper bound for basket and Asian options from vanilla prices alone: price the payoff as if all components were comonotonic, which equals the cheapest static portfolio of vanilla calls that super-replicates it.
- [[derman-et-al-1995-static-options-replication]]: Derman, Ergener and Kani's recipe for hedging a barrier option with a fixed portfolio of vanillas whose value matches the exotic on its boundaries, worked through on an up-and-out call, with the exact put-call-symmetry hedges for special cases.
- [[howison-2006-barrier-options]]: Barrier options as Black–Scholes boundary-value problems: the reflection principle, a catalogue of barrier, touch, ladder, shout and Parisian contracts, and static hedges with vanillas (the author's own preliminary notes).
- [[kirkby-deng-2019-static-hedging-and-pricing-of-exotic-options-with-payoff-frames]]: Static replication with only the strikes that trade: approximate any payoff with a basis of butterflies and get closed-form hedge weights, applied to power payoffs, variance swaps and semi-static barrier hedges.
- [[lipton-sepp-2022-hybrid-method-for-pricing-barrier-options-with-stochastic-volatility]]: Lipton and Sepp price barrier options under stochastic volatility by conditioning on the variance path: Monte Carlo over volatility outside, a one-dimensional barrier problem solved by heat potentials inside, extending Willard's formula to barriers.
- [[rbc-gam-2014-optionality-understanding-callable-bonds]]: An asset manager's primer on callable bonds as a bond plus a written call: call styles, call, rate and reinvestment risk, yield to worst and option-adjusted spread, with one 2007–2011 comparison against bullets.
- [[schmock-et-al-2001-dealing-with-dangerous-digitals]]: Pricing digitals, reverse knock-outs and one-touches at the cost of super-replicating them with a cap on hedge leverage, which explains why such options trade above Black–Scholes and gives the desk habit of shifting the barrier a meaning.
- [[sun-carr-2018-bond-yield-curve-convexity-trading]]: Why the yield curve is usually concave: a zero-cost, zero-duration bond butterfly is long convexity, so if yields were convex in maturity it would also earn positive carry and be an arbitrage (under parallel moves).
