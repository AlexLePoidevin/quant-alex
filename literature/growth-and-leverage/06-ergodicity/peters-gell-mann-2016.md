## Key takeaway

The technical core of the argument. A gamble should be evaluated by the growth rate it
produces for the wealth process it is embedded in, which requires specifying the dynamics
(additive, multiplicative, or otherwise) rather than only the distribution of outcomes.
Under multiplicative dynamics the appropriate transformation is the logarithm — recovering
Bernoulli's answer from dynamics instead of from preferences.

## Why it's here

Read this rather than the popular accounts if you intend to state the argument
precisely. It is also where the claim is at its narrowest and most defensible: a statement
about which transformation makes a multiplicative process ergodic, not a general theory of
choice.
