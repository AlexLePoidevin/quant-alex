## Key takeaway

The mainstream reply. Expected utility theory already handles repeated multiplicative
gambles correctly — dynamic optimisation over a horizon is standard and has been since
Merton — so the ergodicity framing is not identifying an error economics is making. Log
utility is one of many admissible utility functions; asserting it uniquely on dynamical
grounds smuggles in a preference claim rather than eliminating one.

## Why it's here

⚠️ **Include this, and read it before deciding how hard to lean on the ergodicity
framing.** Ergodicity economics is presented in popular writing as a settled correction to
mainstream economics; it is not, and the article will be read by people who know that. The
honest position is narrow and still interesting: the time-average framing is the clearest
*explanation* of why `E[W]` and realised `W` diverge under leverage, and it recovers `f*`
without a utility function — but "which criterion *should* you optimise" is a preference
question that `../02-growth-optimal/samuelson-1979` and `../02-growth-optimal/merton-1969`
had already posed, and neither Peters nor this reply closes it.
