## Key takeaway

Famously written entirely in words of one syllable. The argument: maximising `E[log W]`
is optimal **only if your utility function is log**. A long horizon does not make the log
criterion universally correct — the claim that it does confuses "grows fastest
asymptotically" with "is what a rational investor should want". Someone with different risk
aversion has a different optimum at every horizon, and no amount of time changes that.

## Why it's here

⚠️ **Do not write this article without it.** Kelly has a devoted following and the
literature around it is unusually promotional. Samuelson is the standing objection, it has
never been answered on its own terms, and it is short. Presenting the growth-optimal
criterion without it is advocacy rather than exposition — and the honest resolution is
already in `merton-1969`: pick your `gamma` and say what you picked.
