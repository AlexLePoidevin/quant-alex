## Key takeaway

The St Petersburg paradox and the first use of logarithmic utility. A gamble with
infinite expected value that no one will pay much to play; Bernoulli resolves it by arguing
the *utility* of wealth grows logarithmically, so the expectation that matters is `E[log W]`
rather than `E[W]`.

## Why it's here

**Where the log criterion enters economics, and the text the argument keeps returning to.**
Ergodicity economics (`../06-ergodicity`) claims Bernoulli's insight was correct but got
filed under the wrong heading — as a statement about *preferences* rather than about the
*dynamics* of repeated multiplicative gambles. You cannot evaluate that claim without
having read what Bernoulli actually argued.
