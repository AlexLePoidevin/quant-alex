# Quant Research

My research repository: notes on the sources I read while learning quantitative finance,
kept for my own reference.

It's public in case anyone else wants to learn from it too. Open the literature folder its all there

## House rules for these notes

Adopted because a note that only restates a paper's own claims is worse than no note: it
launders the paper's framing into something that reads like an independent assessment, and
six months later you cannot tell which is which.

**1. Every note carries a `## Where it's weak` section.** Not optional, and not only for
sources judged bad. If a source is strong, write what would have to be true for it to be
wrong. "Nothing obvious" is an acceptable entry; silence is not.

**2. Be critical beyond the conventional reading.** The received summary of a famous paper
is usually its headline number with the conditions stripped off. Record the conditions.
When a result is quoted as a universal constant, check whether it was a calibration.

**3. Separate a source from its citation practice.** A competent paper can support an
inference it never made, which the field then builds on. Note both, separately — the charge
against the paper and the charge against its readers are different charges.

**4. Note incentives.** Who published it, who funded it, and which conclusion their business
favours. This is not an argument against the content; it is a reason to verify rather than
inherit it.

**5. Prefer deriving to citing.** If the result is a line of algebra, do the line. A
derivation tells you more than a citation, arrives with its own error bars, and does not
need trusting. See `literature/growth-and-leverage/03-estimation-error/chopra-ziemba-1993.md`
for the worked case — the one-line sampling distribution of optimal leverage is both exact
and considerably more damning than the paper everyone cites for the same point.

**6. Status is `To read` or `Read`, and it means what it says.** `To read` notes are written
from general knowledge of what a source is known for. Volumes, issues and page numbers in
them are from recall. **Verify before citing in print.**
